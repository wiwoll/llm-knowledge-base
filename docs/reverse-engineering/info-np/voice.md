# Reverse-Engineering Report: Tone of Voice & Editorial Standards — InFo Neurologie & Psychiatrie

**Generated:** 2026-05-01 by parallel agent analysis (Agent 4 of 4).
**Sample base:** NP1-23, NP2-23, NP6-23 (Burggraf era); NP1-24, NP3-24, NP4-24, NP5-24 (Burggraf era); NP1-25, NP2-25, NP3-25, NP4-25 (Burggraf to Schliebe transition); NP5-25, NP6-25, NP1-26 (Blanke era).

## 1. Reader profile (inferred)

**Audience**: Practicing **neurologists AND psychiatrists** in the German-speaking part of **Switzerland (DACH-leaning, but Swiss-anchored)**. Evidence:
- Swiss orthography throughout: "Schliesslich", "ausschliesslich", "grösster", "müssen", "Strasse" (no ß) — see all editorials.
- "Kassenzulässig in der Schweiz", "Spezialitätenliste", "Krankenversicherung KVG" content (NP3-25 p.41).
- Publisher: Prime Public Media AG, Neugasse 10, 8005 Zürich (NP3-25 p.41 Impressum).
- Author affiliations skew Swiss: Chur, Bern, Zürich, Davos, St. Gallen — but German contributors ("Marburg (D)", "Pfaffenhofen (D)", "Köln (D)") appear with explicit "(D)" tag, signalling Swiss-default frame.
- Mix of CME (consultant-level, structured) plus high-level news summaries suggests **resident-to-consultant range**, with Praxismanagement section addressing **private practice**.

**Prior knowledge assumed**: High. Abbreviations like "PIRA", "RAW", "DMT", "NfL", "MoCA", "MDS-UPDRS III", "PCI", "NMDA", "EDSS", "C9orf72", "SOD1" appear without explanation in body text. CME articles do introduce on first use.

**Register**: Formal Hochdeutsch (Swiss), reasonably technical; not as dry as a peer-reviewed journal — leans toward an "engaged colleague briefing colleague" register. Editor era matters: Burggraf was warmer/colloquial; Schliebe was sober-academic; Blanke is conversational-witty.

## 2. Voice fingerprint

### Person & address
- **Reader address with "Sie"** is reserved almost exclusively for **editorials and CME** (e.g., "Wir wünschen Ihnen eine erkenntnisreiche Lektüre", NP3-25 p.3; "Wann haben Sie das letzte Mal etwas zum ersten Mal gemacht?", NP1-25 editorial p.3). News and Medizin items are written impersonally in third person.
- **"Wir" inclusively** appears in Blanke editorials ("Lernen wir schielen", NP6-25 p.3; "wir alle", NP5-25 p.3).
- News items use **impersonal scientific voice**: "In einer Studie wurden …", "Forschende haben gezeigt, dass …" — direct reader address is taboo in news.
- Author byline initials in parentheses signal news-summary authorship: **"(ub)"** for Ulf Blanke, **"(lb)"** for Leoni Burggraf (e.g., NP5-25 p.5: "■(ub) Weltweit sterben jährlich…"; NP1-24 p.20: "■(lb) Multidisziplinäre individualisierte Therapie…"). The black square ■ marks article body start.

### Tense
- **Present tense** dominates background/explanatory passages and recommendations.
- **Perfect/Präteritum** for trial reports: "wurden 116 Patienten eingeschlossen", "konnte gezeigt werden", "Die Ergebnisse zeigten" (NP1-23 p.6; NP3-25 p.7).
- **Conjunctive (Konjunktiv I/II)** for indirect speech reporting expert claims: "Die Wissenschaftler vermuten, dass …" (NP2-23 p.3); "Die Wissenschaftler geben auch zu Bedenken, dass …" (NP4-24 p.3).

### Sentence length
**Long, German-academic-typical** — 25–40+ words is normal in news/CME prose. Editorials slightly shorter, especially Blanke's. Heavy use of nested subordinate clauses with "die", "welche", "wobei". Example (NP3-24 p.6, ~36 words): "Diese Hirnwellen könnten ein Schlüssel zu Lernprozessen im Gehirn sein, denn synchrone Aktivität stärkt die Verbindung."

### Technicality density
- **High in body**: ~3–6 unexplained medical terms per paragraph (PIRA, RAW, EDSS, NfL, mAb, DMT, etc.).
- **Abbreviations introduced once per article** in CME pieces ("Multiplen Sklerose (MS)", "Parkinson (PD)") but assumed known thereafter and across articles.
- News items are denser; CME articles take more time to set up terms.

### Tone valence
- **Neutral-clinical with controlled enthusiasm**. Hedges used carefully and consistently: "möglicherweise", "die Daten deuten darauf hin, dass …", "es ist plausibel, dass …", "scheinen".
- E.g., NP4-25 p.3 (Schliebe): "Damit gilt als plausibel, dass Neuroinflammation an der Krankheitsprogression beteiligt sein könnte – auch wenn Kausalitäten im Detail noch nicht abschliessend geklärt sind."
- Negative results are reported as "Rückschlag" (NP3-25 p.9 ADORE-Studie) — not glossed over.

### Metaphors / colloquialisms
- **Editorials embrace metaphor**, body articles do not. Burggraf: "ein Auge draufhalten" (NP1-23 p.3); "Vorreiter, die vermeintlich wissen, wie es geht" (NP5-24 p.3). Blanke: "Sie schraubt manchmal an Stellen, wo wir noch nicht mal wussten, dass da was lose ist" (NP6-25 p.3).
- News bodies are metaphor-light; occasional headline plays ("Tau-frisch im Alter!?" NP2-23 p.3; "Ein Indianerherz kennt keinen Schmerz – oder doch?" NP6-23 p.3) — Burggraf-era only.

### Humor / lightness
- **Strictly editorial-only under Burggraf and Schliebe**. Under Blanke (NP5-25, NP6-25, NP1-26), wit migrates further into the magazine via his own CME bylines, but news/medizin items remain neutral.
- Blanke example, NP6-25 p.3: "ein schlauer, aber leicht besserwisserische Arzt … braucht nie Kaffee und sagt nie 'Nach meiner Erfahrung der letzten 10 Fälle…', sondern kommt sofort mit 10 000 Studien daher."

### Adjective discipline
- **Hyped vocabulary mostly avoided**. "Bahnbrechend" appears only when quoting an interviewee (NP3-25 p.8: "Die bahnbrechende Entwicklung hat bereits begonnen", in quotes from Prof. Weber).
- "Game-Changer" appears once in quotes: "Tofersen ist ein Gamechanger" (NP3-25 p.8, attributed to Weber).
- "Meilenstein" used sparingly, usually with hedge (NP3-24 p.6: "ein wichtiger Meilenstein").
- Editorial tone of measured optimism: "vielversprechende Ergebnisse" (NP3-25 p.3), "wegweisende Studien" (NP3-25 p.3). Not commercial superlatives.

## 3. Headline patterns

**Two-deck (kicker + main)** structure is universal:
- **Kicker (rubric/topic)** in small caps or coloured: 1–4 words naming the disease/area: "Multiple Sklerose", "Parkinson", "Schmerzen", "Migräne", "Pädiatrische Epilepsie".
- **Main headline**: 4–9 words, descriptive-to-teasing.

Sub-types observed:

**A. Descriptive ("topic-finding") — most common in news/medizin**
- "Lokale Biomarker können schwere Verläufe vorhersagen" (NP3-24 p.7)
- "Neue Endotypen für die personalisierte Therapie detektiert" (NP3-24 p.6)
- "Diazepam-Nasenspray bei Kleinkindern" (NP1-26 p.23)
- "Hirnatrophie korreliert mit Behinderungsprogression bei MS" (NP5-25 TOC)

**B. Question form** — for editorials, occasional medizin
- "Einsatz von Diabetesmedikament für verlangsamten Progress?" (NP3-24 p.7)
- "Echte Chance oder moderne Eintagsfliege?" (NP5-24 p.3)
- "Was bleibt, wenn jemand geht" (NP5-25 p.3 — sub-head only)
- "Kann man Intelligenz schlucken?" (NP1-26 p.6)

**C. Word-play / culturally referenced — Burggraf-era editorial signature**
- "Einmal Psychiatrie, immer Psychiatrie?" (NP1-23 p.3)
- "Tau-frisch im Alter!?" (NP2-23 p.3)
- "Ein Indianerherz kennt keinen Schmerz – oder doch?" (NP6-23 p.3)

**D. Imperative/programmatic** — Blanke editorials
- "Zeit zum Schielen: Mensch vs. Algorithmus" (NP6-25 p.3)

**Punctuation conventions**
- **Colons** as connector: "Tau-frisch im Alter!?", "Zeit zum Schielen: Mensch vs. Algorithmus", "Riluzol: Fortschritt …".
- **En-dash** ("–") for clarification: "Neue Wege gehen – Wagnis oder Freude?" (NP1-25 p.3); "Für eine Zukunft – ohne …".
- **Question marks** common in editorial heads, rare in CME/news.
- No exclamation points except editorial flourish.

**Length**: Most main headlines 5–9 words / 30–60 characters. Editorial heads run longer with subhead.

**Brand vs. generic in headlines**: Generic INN strongly preferred ("Riluzol", "Tofersen", "Lurasidon", "Fremanezumab", "Cannabidiol"). Brand names appear only in Sonderreport/sponsored slots ("Skyclarys™", "Xeplion® / Trevicta®", NP5-25 / NP6-25 TOCs) — clear demarcation.

## 4. Citation style

**Body text**:
- News items: typically **no inline cites**, ending with a "Quelle:" footer pointing to the press release: e.g., NP1-23 p.6: "Quelle: «Neue Hoffnung auf Therapie bei seltener Stoffwechselerkrankung», 20.01.2023, Universität Leipzig (D)". This is the **standard news provenance pattern**.
- CME and Medizin articles: **numbered brackets [1], [2,3], [4,15]** — Vancouver style. E.g., NP3-24 p.9: "negativen Auswirkungen auf die Morbidität und die Mortalität […] [8]"; NP1-24 p.20: "[1]", "[2]", "[3]" referencing DGN Kongress abstracts.
- Studies are sometimes named in narrative ("ATLAS-Studie", "ARISE-Studie", "ADORE-Studie", "CHAIN-Kohortenstudie") with no parenthetical author/year.

**End-of-article reference list**: Numbered, **AMA/Vancouver hybrid**:
- "1. Whitebrook J, et al.: A suicide bereavement model: based on a meta-ethnography of the experiences of adult suicide loss survivors, Front Public Health, 2025; 13: 1596961." (NP5-25 p.6)
- "1. Repantis D, Bovy L, Ohla K, et al.: Cognitive enhancement effects of stimulants… Psychopharmacology 2021; 238(2): 441–451." (NP1-26 p.19)
- DOIs are present when the source is open-access; **PMIDs are not used**. NCT trial registry numbers occasionally appear in CME body but not consistently.

**Press releases**: Always cited as "Quelle: «[release title]», [date], [institution]". Date format: DD.MM.YYYY.

**Conference content**: KONGRESS banner over headline ("KONGRESS DGN 2023", NP1-24 p.20) and references like "Abstract 40. 96. Kongress der Deutschen Gesellschaft für Neurologie. 8.–11. November 2023."

## 5. Drug-name handling

**Observable rule**:
1. **First mention in CME/news**: Generic INN name (Riluzol, Tofersen, Lixisenatid, Teriflunomid, Ocrelizumab, Methylphenidat).
2. **Class/mechanism in apposition**: e.g., NP3-24 p.7: "das Diabetes-Medikament Lixisenatid. Es gehört zur Substanzklasse der GLP-1-(Glucagon-like Peptid-1) Rezeptoragonisten (GLP-1-Analoga)".
3. **Brand names suppressed in editorial content** — they appear only in:
   - Sponsored Sonderreport/Publireportage panels: "OCREVUS®", "Tysabri™", "Skyclarys™", "Xeplion® / Trevicta®", "Tebokan®", "Hyperiplant®", "Zebinix®" — always with ® or ™ and Fachinformation block.
   - Quoted material from interviewees ("Emylif", NP3-25 p.8 — used in quotes).
4. CME = same rule (generic only); news = same; sponsored content = brand-first by definition.

**INN discipline is strong**: even when describing approved Swiss-market drugs, the editorial text uses INN ("Ocrelizumab" not "OCREVUS"). The opposite (brand-first) is a clear marker of paid content.

## 6. Numbers, units, statistics

- **Decimal separator: comma** (Swiss-German convention): "1,53", "1,87", "0,5 mg/kg", "Cohen's d = −0,06", "95 %-KI −0,62 bis −0,06" (NP1-26 p.16; NP2-23 p.3).
- **Thousands separator: thin space** ("100 000 Geburten", "340 000 US-Veteranen", "1 614 Teilnehmer"). Not "100.000" nor "100,000".
- **Percent**: digit + "%" with no space ("46%", "13%", "62%"), occasionally "Prozent" spelled out in editorial-flowing prose ("70 Prozent", "62 und 68%").
- **Confidence intervals**: "95%-Konfidenzintervall −0,24 bis 0,12" or "(95 %-KI −0,62 bis −0,06)" — German "KI" abbreviation.
- **p-values**: less common in news; appear as "p = 0,001" form in CME (no examples in news bodies sampled — news prefers "signifikant", "hochsignifikant").
- **Effect sizes**: HR, OR rare in news prose; appear in CME ("Cohen's d", "Risikoreduktion um 72%").
- **Dosage**: "20 μg", "5 mg", "0,5 mg/kg", "100 mg i.v.", "600 mg alle 6 Monate". μ and Greek letters used.
- **Sample sizes**: "n=78", "n = 1554", inline: "264 Proben von 132 Schlaganfallpatienten".
- **Currency**: Swiss Franken context implied; not numerically prominent in sample.
- **Ranges**: en-dash "−": "1905–1929", "3–5 Monate", "2 bis 5 Jahren".

## 7. Visual conventions

- **"Abb. 1", "Abb. 2"** for figures (Swiss-German abbreviation; not "Fig." or "Bild"). Captions begin with bold descriptive title, then explanatory sentences.
- **"Übersicht 1"** for boxed lists (e.g., NP3-24 p.8 "Übersicht 1: Klinische Fallbeispiele").
- **"Tab. 1"** for tables (less frequent in sample).
- **Take-Home box** = "TAKE-HOME-MESSAGES" rendered in small caps, dash bullets ("―"). Standard CME closer (NP1-23 p.9; NP3-24 p.9).
- Internal cross-refs: "(Abb. 1)", "(Übersicht 1)", "wie in Abbildung 5 gezeigt" (NP1-26 p.15).
- Image credit microtext at margin: "iiievgeniy, istock", "onurdongel, istock", "burcu onter, istock" — lower-case, italicized.

## 8. Recurring rhetorical templates (verbatim quotes)

1. **Editorial closer (Burggraf)**: "Wir wünschen viel Spass bei der Lektüre!" / "Viel Freude bei der Lektüre." (NP1-23 p.3; NP4-24 p.3; NP6-23 p.3)
2. **Editorial closer (Schliebe/Blanke, formal)**: "Ich wünsche Ihnen eine erkenntnisreiche Lektüre der vorliegenden Ausgabe der InFo Neurologie & Psychiatrie." (NP3-25 p.3; NP4-25 p.3; NP5-25 p.3; NP6-25 p.3)
3. **News opener archetype**: "■In einer Studie wurden …" / "■Forschende des … haben …" / "■Eine [britische / amerikanische / interdisziplinäre] Forschergruppe hat nun …" (NP3-24 p.7; NP1-26 p.22)
4. **Editorial signature**: "Leoni Burggraf, Redaktion" / "Tanja Schliebe, Chefredaktion" / "Dr. Ulf Blanke, Redaktion" — three lines, name + role, never decoration.
5. **Trial result phrasing**: "In der … -Studie konnte gezeigt werden, dass …" / "Die Ergebnisse zeigten, dass …" / "Im Ergebnis war …" (NP3-25 p.6; NP6-23 p.6).
6. **Hedged finding**: "Die Daten deuten darauf hin, dass …" (NP3-24 p.7; NP1-23 p.7).
7. **Source attribution**: "Quelle: «[release-title]», DD.MM.YYYY, [institution]" (every news item; NP1-23 p.6 and passim).
8. **CME teaching teaser link**: "> Fortbildungsfragen auf Seite [n]" (NP3-25 p.6, NP1-23 p.8).
9. **Standfirst (Lead-Box)**: 3–5 sentences in distinct text frame above the body, summarising the finding (e.g., NP1-23 p.6: "Die X-chromosomale Adrenoleukodystrophie, eine fortschreitende genetische Stoffwechselerkrankung … kann bisher nicht pharmakologisch behandelt werden. Mit Hilfe eines neuen … könnte der schwere, meist tödliche Verlauf verhindert werden.")
10. **CME affiliation block**: Author name + title block in upper-left margin (Inselspital, Universitätsspital Bern, Freiburgstrasse 20, 3010 Bern, e-mail) — invariant template (NP3-24 p.8).
11. **Conference banner**: "KONGRESS   DGN 2023" (or EAN/AAN) at bottom-right of medizin pieces (NP1-24 p.20).
12. **Take-home dashed bullets**: "― [statement]" — never "•".

## 9. Do's

- Always introduce the topic in a small **kicker (rubric)** above the headline.
- Open every news/medizin body with the **black square ■** glyph, optionally followed by author initials in parentheses ("(ub)", "(lb)").
- Use **Swiss orthography**: ß → ss, Helvetisms permitted ("u.a.", "z.B.", "bzw.", "Spitalambulant", "Kassenzulässig", "Massnahmen").
- Use INN/generic drug names by default; brand names only in clearly marked Sonderreport/Publireportage.
- Cite via numbered brackets [n] in CME prose; provide a "Literatur:" reference list at end.
- Cite news sources with "Quelle: « … », DD.MM.YYYY, Institution".
- Hedge findings: "möglicherweise", "Daten deuten darauf hin", "die Hypothese stützt".
- Close CME articles with a "TAKE-HOME-MESSAGES" box of 3–5 dash-bulleted statements.
- Address reader directly ("Sie", "Ihnen") **only** in editorial and CME framing sentences.
- Sign editorial with "Name, Rolle" — no flourish, no "Mit freundlichen Grüssen".
- Use "Patientinnen und Patienten" or "Personen mit …" forms in newer issues (gender-aware language is normalising from 2024 onward, e.g., NP6-25 p.6).
- Write numbers with comma decimal separator and thin-space thousands.
- Use "Abb." for figures, "Übersicht" for boxed text, "Tab." for tables.

## 10. Don'ts

- **Never** call a drug "revolutionär" / "bahnbrechend" / "Game-Changer" in editorial voice — only inside quotation marks attributing the claim to a named expert.
- **Never** use "wir" in news/medizin items — first-person plural is reserved for editorials and authored CME.
- **Never** lead with a brand name in news/medizin headline or first paragraph (brand-first signals sponsored content).
- **Never** mix single-spaced thousands (1,000) — use thin space (1 000).
- **Never** use period-decimal in numbers (1.5 mg) — use comma (1,5 mg).
- **Never** use "ß"; **never** spell "Strasse" with ß.
- **Never** write a headline ending in an exclamation point (questioning marks OK, !? rare and editor-signature only).
- No emojis, no rhetorical "TL;DR", no English jargon when a German term exists ("Anwendung", not "Application"; but loanwords like "Wearables", "Big Data", "Microdosing" stay English).
- No imperative reader address in news items ("Achten Sie!" — does not appear; reserved for CME closing tips).
- No undisclaimed brand mentions: any brand name comes with ®/™ and a Fachinformation block at page bottom.
- No "ich" in news bodies — editor "ich" only in editorial.
- No anglicised punctuation: German Anführungszeichen "«…»" and "„…"" appear; straight ASCII quotes do not (NP5-24 p.3 "«höher, schneller, weiter»"; NP3-25 p.7 "«Patienten schätzen die einfache Handhabung…»").
- Press releases are not lifted verbatim — always rewritten into magazine voice; the Quelle line credits but does not absolve.

## 11. Quality watermarks (the "feels like InFo NP" tells)

- The black-square **■** + parenthetical author initials opener (e.g., "■(ub) Weltweit sterben…").
- Standfirst-box of 3–5 sentences immediately under the kicker+headline pair, summarising before the body begins.
- Numerical formatting in **Swiss style**: comma decimal, thin-space thousands, no ß.
- "Quelle: «…», DD.MM.YYYY, Institution (D)" — with the country tag.
- "TAKE-HOME-MESSAGES" with em-dash bullets at end of CME pieces.
- Drug names in generic form first, with class in apposition.
- Editorial signature "[Name], Redaktion / Chefredaktion" — two-line, no titles below the line.
- Hedged tone: results "deuten darauf hin", "könnten", "scheinen" rather than "beweisen".
- French Guillemets "«…»" for quoted speech.
- Recurring "Wir wünschen [eine spannende / erkenntnisreiche] Lektüre" close.

## 12. Open issues for human editor input

- **Style guide for "Patientinnen und Patienten" vs. "Patient*innen" vs. "Patient:innen"**: Sample shows all three within the same year (NP3-25 p.4 has "Patient*innen"; NP3-25 p.5 has "Patient:innen"; NP6-25 p.6 has "Patientinnen und Patienten"). House preference unclear.
- **When does Blanke's wit transfer to news/medizin items, vs. stay in editorial only?** Sample shows news bylined "(ub)" still neutral, but the boundary may be softening.
- **Whether KONGRESS banners are mandatory** for all conference-derived content, or only major ones (DGN, EAN, ECTRIMS) — practice is inconsistent.
- **Citation depth threshold**: when does a news item warrant a "Literatur:" list vs. just "Quelle:"? Boundary appears editorial-discretionary.
- **Sponsored content disclosure**: "SONDERREPORT" and "Publireportage" both appear; rules for which label and when remain unclear from text alone.
- **Insider jokes / running gags**: e.g., Burggraf's metaphor-driven editorial heads ("Indianerherz", "Eintagsfliege", "Tau-frisch") may have been a personal signature now retired with the editor change — confirm.
- **Reader-survey data**: % neurologist vs. psychiatrist readers, residency-stage skew — needed for tone calibration.
- **Compact vs. long-form preferences**: NP1-26 ran a single 12-page CME on Neuroenhancement; older issues split into two CMEs of ~6 pages — house policy?
- **Treatment of negative trial results**: "Rückschlag" (NP3-25 p.9) appears uncritically; is there a house preference between euphemism and direct labelling?
